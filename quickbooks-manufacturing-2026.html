QuickBooks manufacturing in 2026: stay on QBO, move to Intuit Enterprise Suite, or add an MRP?

Intuit's manufacturing capability changed in 2026. Small manufacturers should not use an old rule of thumb that "QuickBooks cannot do manufacturing"—but they also should not assume the new manufacturing workflow solves every production-planning problem.

Intuit sources checked Aug 11, 2026Decision scope: small manufacturers
The 2026 change. Intuit now documents manufacturing orders, build assemblies, BOM-driven component requirements, shortage checks and estimated/actual production cost inside Intuit Enterprise Suite. Intuit also states that these manufacturing features are currently available in Intuit Enterprise Suite rather than ordinary QuickBooks Online plans.

What Intuit Enterprise Suite now does

According to Intuit's current help documentation, a manufacturing order can reserve components, show shortages, move through Planned → In Progress → Completed status, and finalize a build that increases finished-goods inventory while consuming components. Estimated and actual costs are tracked through the workflow.

That materially changes the decision for a manufacturer already living in QuickBooks. The real question is no longer simply “QuickBooks or MRP?” It is whether the Enterprise Suite manufacturing model is deep enough for your production complexity, or whether a dedicated MRP still earns its place.

The important limitation: multi-level BOMs

Intuit's current manufacturing setup documentation says multi-level bills of materials with subassemblies are not supported in Intuit Enterprise Suite. That single limitation can become decisive for manufacturers whose products have nested subassemblies, intermediate builds, routings or deeper material-planning dependencies.

Three practical decision paths

PathBest fitMain risk
Stay on ordinary QuickBooks OnlineAccounting-led business with simple inventory and little production planningForcing BOM, production status and material planning into spreadsheets or workarounds
Move to Intuit Enterprise SuiteManufacturer that wants manufacturing execution closer to its finance stack and can work within the documented BOM modelContract pricing and production-depth limits may exceed what a small shop expects from “QuickBooks”
Add a dedicated MRPMulti-level BOM, stronger scheduling, routing, traceability, Shopify/multichannel or more specialized shop-floor needsImplementation, integration and duplicate-master-data risk if finance and manufacturing ownership are not clearly separated

When a dedicated MRP deserves a test

A dedicated MRP should move from “software list” to “pilot candidate” when at least one of these is true:

  • You have multi-level BOMs or subassemblies.
  • Purchasing must react to manufacturing demand rather than simple reorder points.
  • You need routings, work-center capacity or deeper production scheduling.
  • You sell through Shopify or multiple channels and manufacturing inventory must stay synchronized with commercial demand.
  • Lot/serial traceability, expiry, outsourced production or production-specific auditability materially affects operations.

For commerce-led manufacturers, Katana's current Shopify listing emphasizes multi-channel inventory, production planning, purchasing and synchronization across Shopify, Amazon, wholesale and retail. MRPeasy's Shopify listing positions it for small manufacturers up to 200 employees with production planning, BOM, procurement and accounting integrations. Those are different operating models, not interchangeable feature checklists.

Do not choose from a feature matrix alone

The highest-risk transition is often not the software purchase; it is the ownership boundary between finance, inventory, purchasing, production and sales channels. A system can be functionally capable and still fail because SKUs, supplier masters, BOMs, opening stock, units of measure or order ownership are inconsistent.

Our Katana purchasing lab is one example: the useful evidence was not that an API could create a purchase order. It was that invalid supplier/SKU paths could be stopped before a bad write, duplicates could be skipped, and successful requests could write back a visible terminal state.
