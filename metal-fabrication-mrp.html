Do you need inventory software, MRP, or a deeper job-shop ERP?

For a custom shop, the decision usually turns on routings, job costing, material purchasing, WIP visibility, capacity and the depth of BOM/subassembly control—not the longest feature list.

Stay lightweight

Simple jobs, low WIP complexity

If material control is the main constraint and routings/capacity are simple, an inventory-first or light manufacturing system may be enough.

MRP candidate

Recurring BOMs + purchasing + work orders

When shortages, purchasing and production orders interact, test a dedicated MRP path on one representative job family.

Deeper selection

Routings + capacity + job costing

If scheduling, work centers, job costing and deep subassemblies are hard requirements, make them hard gates in vendor evaluation.

Pilot before migration

One representative job can expose most selection mistakes.

  1. Set up one real customer job and its material/BOM structure.
  2. Run purchasing and receiving for one constrained material.
  3. Release work through the actual routing or work-order steps.
  4. Record scrap/rework/WIP movements if they matter operationally.
  5. Close the job and compare expected versus actual material and labor cost.
  6. Test the accounting/export handoff before committing to migration.
