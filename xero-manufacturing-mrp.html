Keep Xero for accounting—or add a manufacturing system?

For many small manufacturers, the decision is not whether to replace Xero. It is whether production, inventory, BOMs, WIP, purchasing and costing have outgrown an accounting-led stack.

Start with the boundary, not the brand. Xero's own current US inventory guidance says manufacturers are likely to need core Xero together with specialized manufacturing inventory apps for BOMs, WIP and production tracking. Xero's App Store separately lists manufacturing and MRP apps that integrate with Xero. That makes "keep Xero + add an operations layer" a first-class architecture, not a workaround.
Stay lightweight

Xero is not the bottleneck

If inventory is reliable, BOMs are simple and production planning is informal but controlled, adding full MRP may create more work than value.

Add manufacturing depth

Operations have outgrown accounting

Recurring shortages, multi-level BOMs, work orders, routings, WIP, capacity or production-costing gaps are signs to test a dedicated manufacturing layer.

Do not assume integration depth

Accounting sync is not all-or-nothing

Different manufacturing systems send different data to Xero. Test invoices, bills, tax mapping, inventory/WIP journals and multi-currency behavior before committing.

Four signals that Xero should remain the accounting layer—but not the operating system

  • Production demand drives purchasing. Buyers need component requirements tied to orders or forecasts, not only reorder points.
  • WIP matters. Materials move through production before becoming finished goods and the business needs that state to be visible and costed.
  • BOM depth matters. Subassemblies, revisions, routings or dependent demand make spreadsheet planning fragile.
  • Multiple locations or deeper warehouse control matter. Xero's current US documentation says core Xero does not track inventory across multiple locations and points users toward Inventory Plus or third-party apps.

None of these proves that a full ERP is required. Often the cleaner boundary is Xero for accounting plus a manufacturing or inventory system for operational truth.

Representative pilot: one order end to end

  1. Create one real customer order and product/BOM.
  2. Buy and receive one constrained material.
  3. Release and partially complete production.
  4. Record WIP, scrap/rework or labor if those matter.
  5. Finish and ship the order.
  6. Push the relevant accounting documents to Xero.
  7. Check tax, currency, inventory/WIP and COGS treatment with your accountant.
  8. Export the core data you would need if you later leave the system.
