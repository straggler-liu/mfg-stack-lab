Buying-moment decision guide

Do you need inventory software, MRP, or ERP?

Buy the smallest system that can reliably control the decisions your current stack cannot. The boundary is driven by workflow complexity, not feature count.

Map my boundaryRun Quick Fit
Inventory-first

Stay lightweight when stock is the main problem

Best fit when the hard decisions are purchasing, receiving, locations, replenishment and basic assembly rather than production planning.

MRP boundary

Move to MRP when production drives purchasing

MRP becomes more useful when multi-level BOMs, work orders, material availability, production timing or job/product cost must stay synchronized.

ERP boundary

Move deeper when cross-functional control requires it

ERP is easier to justify when manufacturing must share one governed transaction model with finance, multi-entity operations, complex approvals or advanced warehousing.

Five workflow tests

Run these before vendor demos.

1. Demand to material: Can one real demand signal explode the correct BOM, net stock and create an explainable material requirement?

2. Material to production: Can a planner see what can start now, what is blocked and why without rebuilding the answer in Excel?

3. Production to WIP: Can the team record issue, completion, scrap and status at the required control depth?

4. Production to cost: Can the business get the costing accuracy it needs without offline reconciliation?

5. Production to other systems: Can accounting, purchasing and required operational systems exchange the right transactions without duplicate ownership?

If a lighter system passes all five at the required control depth, a larger ERP may add cost without adding decision quality.

Hard non-fit boundaries

Do not buy a bigger system for the wrong reason.

Bad master data

If BOMs, items, units or lead times are unreliable, a new system can automate bad inputs faster.

Weak process ownership

If nobody owns planning, purchasing rules or transaction discipline, replacement can reproduce the same ambiguity at higher cost.

Integration-only pain

If the current MRP is adequate but data is trapped between systems, stabilize the integration boundary before replacing the core.

Facts that should change the recommendation

Manufacturing model; BOM levels and revisions; planning/capacity needs; lot/serial traceability; job/product costing; current accounting/inventory stack; required integrations; migration timeline and implementation ownership.
